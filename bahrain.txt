BAHRAIN
Official language: Arabic

TITLE: "Yalla Bahrain" (Come On, Bahrain)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Khaleeji-fusion stadium anthem. Oud and mesmar (Gulf reed horn) trading the
lead melody over a driving khaliji hand-drum rhythm, synth bass and a
four-on-the-floor kick lifting the chorus into pop-rock territory, night-race
engine-roar sound design as a texture in the bridge. Call-and-response
between a lead male vocal and a massed crowd chant.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Oud figure over distant crowd murmur, then engines roar past)
Yalla... yalla, Bahrain...

[Verse 1] (Arabic hook woven through English narrative)
Before the bridges, before the lights came on,
Our grandfathers dove for pearls where the tide is gone,
Now the causeway hums and the desert turns to flame,
Every Friday night this island shouts one name.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the oyster bed to the roaring headlights track,
Small island, big heart — we never look back.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Arabic + English)
Yalla Bahrain! Yalla Bahrain!
(Come on, Bahrain! Come on, Bahrain!)
Habibi qum, Yalla Bahrain!
(My love, rise up — come on, Bahrain!)

[Verse 2]
Manama's skyline burning gold against the sea,
An island built on divers' breath and family,
The whole Gulf watches when our colors take the field,
Small on the map, but our roar is never sealed.

[Chorus]
Yalla Bahrain! Yalla Bahrain!
Habibi qum, Yalla Bahrain!

[Bridge]
(Engine roar swells, then cuts to a cappella claps)
No pearl too deep, no lap too far to run,
We are the island that outlasts the sun.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Yalla Bahrain! Yalla Bahrain!
Habibi qum, Yalla Bahrain!
Yalla! Yalla! Bahrain!

[Outro — chant tag]
Yalla, yalla, Bahrain!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Anchors the story in Bahrain's actual pearl-diving heritage (the pre-oil
economy and identity of the islands), the King Fahd Causeway connecting it
to the mainland, and the Bahrain International Circuit's Grand Prix
night races — a genuinely distinctive global sporting hook for such a small
country. "Yalla" ("come on/let's go") is a real, widely used Gulf Arabic
expression, making the hook natural to chant. Have a native Bahraini Arabic
speaker review the phrasing before recording.
