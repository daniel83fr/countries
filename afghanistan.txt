AFGHANISTAN
Official language(s): Pashto, Dari

TITLE: "Watan Zamung" (This Homeland Is Ours)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Afghan Attan-fusion stadium anthem. Rubab lead melody over a driving daf and
zerbaghali hand-drum pulse, harmonium drone underneath, tempo lifting from a
slow 4/4 intro into a fast 6/8 Attan spin for the choruses. Male and female
unison lead, massed male crowd-chant backing vocals, hand-claps on the
off-beat. Think: traditional Kabul wedding music colliding with a football
terrace chant.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Solo rubab rises out of silence over a slow heartbeat daf)
Da ghro sitara... (The stars over the mountains...)

[Verse 1]
In the shadow of the Hindu Kush the boys are counting runs,
Kites like scraps of color chasing after the sun,
From Kandahar dust to Kabul's broken road the bat still swings,
Every wicket that falls, a whole nation sings.

[Pre-Chorus]
Pashtun, Tajik, Hazara, Uzbek — one flag, one home,
Whatever the years have taken, we still call this our own.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Pashto + English)
Watan zamung dey! Watan zamung dey!
(This homeland is ours! This homeland is ours!)
Da zamung watan! Da zamung watan!
Stand up, Afghanistan!

[Verse 2]
The rubab was your grandfather's, now it's yours to carry on,
Every string still humming with the ones who came before and gone,
When the last wicket falls and the whole ground goes loud,
We are not the rubble — we are the mountain, we are the cloud.

[Chorus]
Watan zamung dey! Watan zamung dey!
Da zamung watan! Da zamung watan!
Stand up, Afghanistan!

[Bridge]
Let the daf beat like a hundred hearts as one,
Let the kite climb higher than the setting sun —
We buried nothing that we can't rebuild,
This ground is ours, and this ground will yield.

[Final Chorus — full crowd, gang vocals]
Watan zamung dey! Watan zamung dey!
Da zamung watan! Da zamung watan!
Stand up, Afghanistan! Stand up, Afghanistan!

[Outro — chant tag]
Watan! Watan! Da zamung watan!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built around two real, specific Afghan touchstones: the rubab (the national
instrument, passed down through families) and the country's improbable rise
as a cricketing nation loved across ethnic lines despite decades of
hardship — plus the kite, a genuine and beloved piece of Afghan street
culture. "Watan" (homeland) is a real, widely understood word in both Pashto
and Dari, chosen deliberately so the hook works across the country's main
language groups. NOTE FOR PRODUCTION: verses are written in English for
storytelling precision; the Pashto/Dari chorus lines should be reviewed by a
native speaker/lyricist before recording to polish idiom and meter.
