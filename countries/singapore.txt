SINGAPORE
Official languages: Malay, English, Mandarin, Tamil

TITLE: "Majulah Singapura" (Onward, Singapore)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Multicultural Singaporean stadium anthem. Gamelan gong and kompang drums
open alongside erhu and tabla, three musical heritages trading phrases
before merging into a single full-band pop-rock chorus with massed crowd
vocals — the sound of a hawker-center night market scaled up to a stadium.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Gamelan gong hit answered by tabla and erhu in turn, then together)
Majulah... Singapura...

[Verse 1]
Merlion spitting harbor spray against the skyline's glow,
Hawker smoke from four cuisines rising row by row,
A fishing village grown into a city built on trust,
Every language on this island cheering for the same "must."

[Pre-Chorus]
From the Marina Bay lights to the market's crowded floor,
One island, four tongues, singing for one shore.

[Chorus] (viral hook — the real national anthem's own title phrase)
Majulah Singapura! Majulah Singapura!
(Onward, Singapore! Onward, Singapore!)
Bersama kita maju — Majulah Singapura!
(Together we advance — onward, Singapore!)

[Verse 2]
Uncle at the hawker stall, auntie in the void deck hall,
Watching the same jersey on the same screen through it all,
Doesn't matter what your grandmother's tongue was passed to you,
When the whole crowd sings as one, every word rings true.

[Chorus]
Majulah Singapura! Majulah Singapura!
Bersama kita maju — Majulah Singapura!

[Bridge]
Four tongues, one island, standing side by side,
A fishing port that became the whole world's pride —
Merlion roaring louder than the tide,
This is the anthem this small island rides.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Majulah Singapura! Majulah Singapura!
Bersama kita maju — Majulah Singapura!
Majulah! Majulah!

[Outro — chant tag]
Majulah Singapura!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built directly on "Majulah Singapura" ("Onward, Singapore"), the actual
title and refrain of Singapore's national anthem in Malay — an already
deeply recognized phrase — paired with the Merlion and the hawker-center
culture that blends the island's Malay, Chinese, Indian, and Eurasian
heritage into daily life. Have a native Malay speaker confirm phrasing
before recording.
