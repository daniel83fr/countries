ENGLAND
Official language: English

TITLE: "Three Lions on the Shirt"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Old-school football terrace chant fused with a brass-band pub-anthem swell — the sound of a working-men's-club singalong crossed with a Wembley crowd: thumping bass drum on the terrace beat, a battered upright-piano-and-brass hook straight out of a match-day pub, and a massed, slightly out-of-tune male-voice choir chorus built entirely to be bellowed, not sung pretty.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Brass band strikes up, terrace clapping, one voice starts it off)
Ohhh... England... England...

[Verse 1]
Three lions on the shirt, same badge my old man wore,
Wembley's twin towers stood watching long before,
We've sung through the low times, sung through all the pain,
Get in the ground, stand together, sing it out again.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the terraces to the tube train home,
Every corner of this country, every empty stadium seat, one throne.

[Chorus]
England! England!
Three lions on the shirt, we're never gonna stop,
England! England!
Sing till the final whistle, sing until we drop.

[Verse 2]
Under the new arch now where the old towers used to stand,
Same songs, same scarves, same beer in the same hand,
Doesn't matter what the tabloids say about our chance,
Ninety minutes, all together, up and down we dance.

[Chorus]
England! England!
Three lions on the shirt, we're never gonna stop,
England! England!
Sing till the final whistle, sing until we drop.

[Bridge]
Brass band blaring, bass drum on the beat,
Every pub in every town stamping on the street,
Three lions roaring since Ninety-Six and before,
Football's coming home, that's what we're singing for.

[Final Chorus]
England! England!
Three lions on the shirt, we're never gonna stop,
England! England!
Sing until the whistle blows, sing until we drop.

[Outro]
Three lions! Three lions! England!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the real Three Lions crest worn on England's football shirt since 1872, and the terrace-chant/brass-band pub culture that has carried English football songs for generations — call-and-response singing born on standing terraces, later immortalized in the real "Three Lions" anthem tradition. The lyric also nods to Wembley Stadium's original Twin Towers (demolished 2003) and the new stadium's arch that replaced them as the enduring symbol fans sing about.
