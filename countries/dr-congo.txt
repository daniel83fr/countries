DR CONGO
Official language: French (Lingala widely spoken)

TITLE: "Congo, Tokende" (Congo, Let's Go)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Soukous-driven stadium anthem. Fast, interlocking rumba/soukous guitar
lines (the genre DR Congo gave the entire African continent) over a
conga-and-shaker groove, sebene guitar breakdowns building tension before
each chorus, massed crowd vocals in Lingala call-and-response.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Soukous guitar riff locks in over a rising conga pattern)
Congo... tokende...

[Verse 1]
The river runs so wide it forgets to ever end,
A thousand different voices that this one flag can still befriend,
Gorillas move like shadows through the Virunga misted green,
And every guitar solo is the loudest thing you've seen.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the river's mighty current to the stadium's bright glow,
Two hundred voices, one same rhythm, ready to go.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Lingala + English)
Congo, tokende! Congo, tokende!
(Congo, let's go! Congo, let's go!)
Biso nyonso — Congo, tokende!
(All of us — Congo, let's go!)

[Verse 2]
The Leopards move across the pitch the way the guitar solos climb,
Fast, unstoppable, and always exactly right on time,
Doesn't matter how many nations doubt what we can do,
This river-nation always finds a rhythm to break through.

[Chorus]
Congo, tokende! Congo, tokende!
Biso nyonso — Congo, tokende!

[Bridge]
Guitar string bending like the river's endless bend,
Two hundred voices singing that this never has to end —
Sebene solo building to the sky,
This is the anthem of a nation flying high.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Congo, tokende! Congo, tokende!
Biso nyonso — Congo, tokende!
Tokende! Tokende!

[Outro — chant tag]
Congo! Tokende!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on soukous/rumba congolaise, the guitar-driven genre that began on
the Congo River's banks and spread across all of Africa, and the Virunga
gorillas, a real and globally recognized natural treasure. The Leopards is
the actual nickname of DR Congo's national football team. Have a native
Lingala speaker review phrasing before recording.
