SCOTLAND
Official language: English (Scots and Scottish Gaelic also spoken)

TITLE: "Tartan Roar"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Bagpipe-and-stadium-rock anthem. Highland pipes open solo with a
piobaireachd-style flourish, then a snare drum kicks in a Highland-fling
strathspey rhythm before a full rock band — electric guitar, bass, kit —
slams in underneath, building to a massed terrace chorus meant to be
bellowed the way "Flower of Scotland" already is before every match.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Lone piper plays, then a snare rattles into a fling beat)
Here we go, here we go, the Tartan Army's on the road...

[Verse 1]
Fae Glesga tae the Highlands, ye can hear the same auld cry,
Every wee toun and city stands thegither, dinnae ask us why,
The pipes dinnae care what the odds are on the wall,
We've aye turned up loud and proud, whitever daurs befall.

[Pre-Chorus]
Roll the drum, gie it laldy, let the pipes sing oot and high,
This is oor land, oor team, oor time, sae dinnae ask us why.

[Chorus] — the massed terrace hook
Scotland! Scotland!
Tartan Army, roar it loud and clear!
Scotland! Scotland!
We'll sing this song forever, hen, dinnae you fear!

[Verse 2]
Doesnae matter if we're big or if we're sma' on paper,
The Tartan Army follows through the wind and through the rain,
Fae Hampden tae a hunder touns across the world we've travelled,
Ae roar, ae song, ae nation, aye and aye again.

[Chorus]
Scotland! Scotland!
Tartan Army, roar it loud and clear!
Scotland! Scotland!
We'll sing this song forever, hen, dinnae you fear!

[Bridge]
The pipes are skirlin' high above the roar o' the stand,
Ilka voice thegither, haudin' firm, haudin' the land,
Nae matter whit the scoreboard says when the final whistle blaws,
The Tartan Army sings on, and that's the reason we've aw stood tall.

[Final Chorus]
Scotland! Scotland!
Tartan Army, roar it loud and clear!

[Outro]
Here's tae Scotland — roar it, hen, roar it!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the Tartan Army, the real, beloved nickname for Scotland's
football support, famous worldwide for travelling in numbers and singing
through defeats as loudly as wins. The music fuses genuine Highland
bagpipe tradition (piobaireachd solo, strathspey/fling rhythm) with
stadium rock, echoing the real crowd-singing power of "Flower of
Scotland" at Hampden Park. The lyric leans hard into real Scots dialect
words (laldy, gie it, skirlin', ilka, haudin', dinnae, hen) for
authenticity rather than generic English.
