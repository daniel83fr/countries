NEW ZEALAND
Official languages: English, Māori, New Zealand Sign Language

TITLE: "Ninety-First Minute"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Stadium rock built on a haka cadence. Stamping, unison waewae-takahia
foot-percussion and low massed chanting open the track before it breaks
into a driving guitar-and-drum stadium-rock chorus built for a full
Westpac Stadium crowd.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Feet stamp in unison, low chant rises, guitar cuts in on the downbeat)
All Whites... All Whites... ka mate, ka ora...

[Verse 1]
Slovakia scored early, the clock was running out,
Winston Reid rose up and silenced every doubt,
Ninety-first minute header, Wellington went wild,
That's the night the whole small nation felt like David's child.

[Pre-Chorus]
Kia kaha, stand up strong, the whistle's about to blow,
Unbeaten through South Africa, that's a story we still know.

[Chorus]
All Whites! All Whites!
Ninety-first minute, we never say we're done,
All Whites! All Whites!
Small nation, big heart, watch the long white cloud outrun.

[Verse 2]
Rory Fallon's header sent us to the world's biggest stage,
Every up-and-comer learns that story page by page,
Haka stamp before the whistle, silence before the roar,
This is a small island proving heart counts more than score.

[Chorus]
All Whites! All Whites!
Ninety-first minute, we never say we're done,
All Whites! All Whites!
Small nation, big heart, watch the long white cloud outrun.

[Bridge]
Kia kaha, ka mate, ka ora, stamping feet as one,
From Aotearoa's coast to where the whistle's blown,
Three draws, no losses, a whole nation stood as tall,
Ninety-first minute taught us heart outlasts them all.

[Final Chorus]
All Whites! All Whites!
Small nation, big heart, watch the long white cloud outrun.

[Outro]
Kia kaha! All Whites! Aotearoa!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on two real All Whites moments: Rory Fallon's headed goal against
Bahrain that sent New Zealand to the 2010 World Cup, and Winston Reid's
91st-minute equalizer against Slovakia in that tournament's opening match,
part of the run that saw New Zealand finish the World Cup unbeaten. "Kia
kaha" (stand strong) is a genuine Māori phrase used nationwide as a rallying
call, and "Aotearoa" is the Māori name for New Zealand.
