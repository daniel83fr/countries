COOK ISLANDS
Official languages: English, Cook Islands Māori

TITLE: "Pate for Rarotonga"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Ura pate stadium anthem. Built on the pate — the slit log drum central to
Cook Islands dance competitions like Te Maeva Nui — layering fast, rolling
drum patterns under call-and-response crowd vocals, with ukulele fills
carrying the melodic hook.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Pate drums roll in a fast rolling pattern, ukulele answers)
Kia orana... Rarotonga... e tuki, e tuki...

[Verse 1]
Muri lagoon sits still before the whistle starts to blow,
Every drummer on this island knows exactly how to go,
Te Maeva Nui taught our hands this same relentless beat,
Now the stadium is the stage and the pitch is our own street.

[Pre-Chorus]
Aitutaki to Avarua, every island sends its sound,
One small nation, twelve small islands, one same holy ground.

[Chorus]
Kia manuia! Rarotonga, e tuki!
Pate drumming, we are small but we are loud and true,
Kia manuia! Rarotonga, e tuki!
Kūki 'Āirani, watch what twelve small islands do.

[Verse 2]
Tivaevae stitched with colors that our mothers chose by hand,
Every stitch a story tying player back to land,
Ura dancers move their hips the way our strikers move the ball,
Same old rhythm, brand new stadium, but the drum outlasts them all.

[Chorus]
Kia manuia! Rarotonga, e tuki!
Pate drumming, we are small but we are loud and true,
Kia manuia! Rarotonga, e tuki!
Kūki 'Āirani, watch what twelve small islands do.

[Bridge]
E tuki, e tuki, hands upon the log drum's skin,
Twelve small islands breathing as one when we begin,
Kia orana to the ones who came before,
Every goal we score here echoes off Muri's shore.

[Final Chorus]
Kia manuia! Rarotonga, e tuki!
Kūki 'Āirani, watch what twelve small islands do.

[Outro]
Kia orana! Kūki 'Āirani! E tuki!

CULTURAL NOTE:
The pate (slit log drum) and the fast, competitive drumming showcased at
Te Maeva Nui — the Cook Islands' annual constitution-week dance and drum
festival — are the real backbone of Cook Islands performance culture, and
"Kūki 'Āirani" is the Cook Islands Māori name for the nation itself. The
tivaevae (appliqued quilt) reference points to another genuinely
hand-passed island craft tradition woven into everyday Cook Islands life.
