MYANMAR
Official language: Burmese

TITLE: "Myanmar, Aung Pa Say" (Myanmar, May You Be Victorious)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Saung-harp stadium anthem. Saung (Burmese arched harp) melody opening the
verses over a hsaing waing gong-circle rhythm, chinlone-inspired
interlocking percussion patterns (echoing the rhythm players use around a
chinlone circle), building into a full-band chorus with massed crowd
vocals under a golden pagoda skyline.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Saung harp arpeggio over a soft gong-circle pulse)
Myanmar... aung pa say...

[Verse 1]
Golden stupas catching sunset over the Irrawaddy's bend,
A rattan ball kept in the air by strangers turned to friends,
Chinlone never needs a winner, only rhythm, only grace,
But tonight eleven players carry pride onto the pitch's face.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the temple bells at evening to the roar under the lights,
One nation finds its rhythm on the biggest of nights.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Burmese + English)
Myanmar, aung pa say! Myanmar, aung pa say!
(Myanmar, may you be victorious!)
Anaeh, Myanmar, aung pa say!
(With strength, Myanmar, be victorious!)

[Verse 2]
The circle of chinlone taught us to play for one another first,
That rhythm shared is stronger than a single trophy's worth,
Now the whole stadium moves together like a circle wide,
Every foot, every heartbeat, keeping the same ball alive.

[Chorus]
Myanmar, aung pa say! Myanmar, aung pa say!
Anaeh, Myanmar, aung pa say!

[Bridge]
Golden pagoda, silver river, rattan ball in flight,
Grace before glory, that's how we hold the light —
Harp string and gong and a hundred feet as one,
This is the anthem for what we've already won.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Myanmar, aung pa say! Myanmar, aung pa say!
Anaeh, Myanmar, aung pa say!
Aung pa say! Aung pa say!

[Outro — chant tag]
Myanmar! Aung pa say!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Centers on chinlone, Myanmar's traditional non-competitive game of keeping
a rattan ball aloft as a group — a genuinely distinctive, beautiful piece
of Burmese sporting culture — plus the saung harp and the country's
golden-pagoda landscape. Have a native Burmese speaker review phrasing
before recording.
