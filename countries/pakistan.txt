PAKISTAN
Official languages: Urdu, English

TITLE: "Pakistan Zindabad" (Long Live Pakistan)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Qawwali-fusion stadium anthem. Tabla and dholak driving the rhythm,
harmonium runs and hand-claps building the qawwali call-and-response,
exploding into a full-band arena chorus with massed crowd vocals — the
energy of a packed National Stadium turned into a record.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Harmonium drone rises under a rolling dholak pattern)
Pakistan... zindabad...

[Verse 1]
K2 stands taller than most nations dare to dream they'd grow,
The Indus carried empires long before this flag would fly or show,
A street cricket match played with a bat carved out of wood and pride,
Every kid in every alley has the same team on their side.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the Karakoram's shadow to the Arabian tide,
One nation, green and white, standing side by side.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Urdu + English — an actual iconic national chant)
Pakistan, zindabad! Pakistan, zindabad!
(Long live Pakistan! Long live Pakistan!)
Sabse pehle, Pakistan zindabad!
(Pakistan first, long live Pakistan!)

[Verse 2]
The qawwal's hand-clap sets the pace before the bowler runs,
A billion different rooftops watching for the winning run,
Doesn't matter what the years or scoreboards say we've lost,
We built a nation out of mountains — we know what things cost.

[Chorus]
Pakistan, zindabad! Pakistan, zindabad!
Sabse pehle, Pakistan zindabad!

[Bridge]
Green for the fields and white for the peace we've claimed,
K2's shadow taught us we don't play afraid —
Tabla and hand-clap, dholak and the crowd,
This is the anthem that Pakistan sings out loud.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Pakistan, zindabad! Pakistan, zindabad!
Sabse pehle, Pakistan zindabad!
Zindabad! Zindabad!

[Outro — chant tag]
Pakistan Zindabad!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on "Pakistan Zindabad" ("Long live Pakistan"), a genuine, deeply
iconic national chant, alongside K2 and the Indus River — real,
non-generic landmarks — plus the qawwali hand-clap tradition and street
cricket, the country's true sporting religion. Have a native Urdu speaker
review phrasing before recording.
