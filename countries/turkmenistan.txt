TURKMENISTAN
Official language: Turkmen

TITLE: "Türkmenistan, Öňe!" (Turkmenistan, Forward!)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Dutar-driven steppe anthem. Dutar lute melody carrying the hook over a
galloping frame-drum rhythm built to evoke the Akhal-Teke horse's stride,
bakhshi throat-singing texture in the verses, opening into a full-band
chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Dutar lute figure over a slow galloping drum pulse)
Türkmenistan... öňe...

[Verse 1]
Golden horses run the Karakum like they were born from light,
Every carpet ever woven holds a story in its sight,
Bakhshi singers carried history before a page was made,
Every thread, every gallop, is a promise that won't fade.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the desert dune at sunset to the stadium's open door,
One nation, gold and patient, carrying more.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Turkmen + English)
Türkmenistan, öňe! Türkmenistan, öňe!
(Turkmenistan, forward! Turkmenistan, forward!)
Bile, bile — Türkmenistan, öňe!
(Together, together — Turkmenistan, forward!)

[Verse 2]
The Akhal-Teke's coat still catches sunlight like spun gold,
A horse bred here for centuries, more precious than we're told,
When the whole stand rises for the whistle and the run,
We carry the same fire that the ancient riders spun.

[Chorus]
Türkmenistan, öňe! Türkmenistan, öňe!
Bile, bile — Türkmenistan, öňe!

[Bridge]
Carpet thread and horse's mane, woven side by side,
Desert patience carries its own kind of pride —
Dutar string and drumbeat, running as one,
This is the anthem beneath the desert sun.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Türkmenistan, öňe! Türkmenistan, öňe!
Bile, bile — Türkmenistan, öňe!
Öňe! Öňe!

[Outro — chant tag]
Türkmenistan! Öňe!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the Akhal-Teke, Turkmenistan's legendary golden horse breed and a
genuine source of deep national pride, plus the country's famous carpet-
weaving tradition (patterns from which appear on the national flag). "Öňe"
(forward) is a real Turkmen word for rallying people on. Have a native
Turkmen speaker review phrasing before recording.
