BARBADOS
Official language: English

TITLE: "Crop Over Roar"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Crop Over soca stadium anthem. Tuk band rhythm (Barbados's own fife-and-
drum tradition) opens the verses, building into a full soca groove for the
chorus — the sound of the Crop Over festival's Grand Kadooment parade
scaled to a stadium.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Tuk band fife and drum build under a rising soca groove)
Barbados... roar...

[Verse 1]
Sugarcane taught this island rhythm long before the soca came,
Flying fish and cou-cou served the same way, just the same,
Cricket taught us patience swinging underneath the island sun,
And every match-day here still carries the beat that we've become.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the cane field's swaying green to the stadium's roaring crowd,
One small island, first republic, standing proud.

[Chorus]
Crop Over roar! Crop Over roar!
Tuk band rhythm shaking every open door,
Crop Over roar! Crop Over roar!
Barbados, Barbados, hear the whole island roar.

[Verse 2]
The boundary that we've hit still echoes off the cane field's edge,
Every legend of this island cut from the very same ledge,
Doesn't matter how the bigger nations count the years,
We turned an island's history into a hundred cheers.

[Chorus]
Crop Over roar! Crop Over roar!
Tuk band rhythm shaking every open door,
Crop Over roar! Crop Over roar!
Barbados, Barbados, hear the whole island roar.

[Bridge]
Tuk band drum still beating like the cane field's sway,
An island proud of every single thing it built its way —
Soca groove and cricket ball as one,
This is the anthem underneath the sun.

[Final Chorus]
Crop Over roar! Crop Over roar!
Barbados, Barbados, hear the whole island roar.

[Outro]
Barbados! Crop Over! Roar!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on Crop Over, Barbados's real annual sugarcane-harvest festival, tuk
band music (a genuine local fife-and-drum tradition), and the island's
deep, real cricketing legacy. English is Barbados's official language, so
the full lyric is written directly in it.
