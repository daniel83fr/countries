CHINESE TAIPEI
Official language: Mandarin Chinese

TITLE: "Zhonghua Jiayou" (Go, Chinese Taipei)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Taiwanese night-market stadium anthem. Erhu and suona horn melody over a
baseball-stadium clap-and-drum rhythm (the same call-and-response energy
as a CPBL crowd), electric guitar and synths lifting into the chorus,
temple-festival gongs and firecracker-style percussion hits as accents.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Suona horn blast over a stadium clap track)
Zhonghua Taipei... jiayou...

[Verse 1]
Night market steam rising over neon-colored streets,
A stadium of scooters parked for nine full innings' heat,
Taroko's cliffs stand taller than a mountain has to be,
And every plum blossom still blooms after the coldest sea.

[Pre-Chorus]
From Yushan's peak down to the harbor's glowing light,
This island's heartbeat never rests, not even at night.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Mandarin + English)
Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou! Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou!
(Chinese Taipei, go! Chinese Taipei, go!)
Da jia yiqi jiayou!
(Everyone, add oil together!)

[Verse 2]
Grandma's fan still waves the rhythm every summer game,
The crowd claps out a heartbeat that no scoreboard can tame,
Small island, big shout, echoing off every hill,
When the ninth inning comes, nobody's sitting still.

[Chorus]
Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou! Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou!
Da jia yiqi jiayou!

[Bridge]
The plum blossom opens when the winter's at its worst,
This island learned to bloom by going through it first —
Whatever the name on the scoreboard light,
We know exactly who we are tonight.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou! Zhonghua Taipei, jiayou!
Da jia yiqi jiayou!
Jiayou! Jiayou!

[Outro — chant tag]
Jiayou! Jiayou! Zhonghua Taipei!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Leans on the deep, genuine local passion for baseball (the island's true
national sport and social ritual) and specific landmarks — Taroko Gorge,
Yushan (the island's highest peak), and the plum blossom (the national
flower, prized for blooming through winter cold as a symbol of
resilience). Uses "Zhonghua Taipei" (中華台北), the actual name fans use when
cheering under this designation, so it's authentic rather than politically
loaded. Have a native Taiwanese Mandarin speaker review phrasing before
recording.
