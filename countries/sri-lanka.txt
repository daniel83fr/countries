SRI LANKA
Official languages: Sinhala, Tamil

TITLE: "Jayawewa, Sri Lanka" (Victory, Sri Lanka)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Kandyan-fusion stadium anthem. Geta bera drums (the ceremonial Kandyan
dance drum) driving the rhythm, veena and flute melody lines, building
into a full-band arena chorus that carries both Sinhala and Tamil victory
phrases side by side, honoring the island's two official languages.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Geta bera drum roll builds over temple bell chimes)
Jayawewa... jayawewa...

[Verse 1]
Elephants pass beneath stupas older than the written page,
Tea leaves picked by hand still color every mountain stage,
Eighteen years remembered from a single World Cup night,
When a whole small island proved that underdogs can fight.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the tea hills of the highland to the golden beach below,
One island, two great languages, one same afterglow.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Sinhala + Tamil + English)
Jayawewa, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!
(Victory, Sri Lanka! Victory, Sri Lanka!)
Vaazhga, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!
(Long live, Sri Lanka! Victory, Sri Lanka!)

[Verse 2]
The Kandyan drummer's hands move faster than the eye can trace,
Every dancer, every cricketer inherits the same grace,
Doesn't matter what the scoreboard read a decade or two back,
This island learned to rise from every single setback.

[Chorus]
Jayawewa, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!
Vaazhga, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!

[Bridge]
Drum and dance and two tongues singing as one,
Island in the ocean, never done —
Elephant and stupa, tea leaf and the sea,
This is the anthem of an island proud and free.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Jayawewa, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!
Vaazhga, Sri Lanka! Jayawewa, Sri Lanka!

[Outro — chant tag]
Jayawewa! Vaazhga! Sri Lanka!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Anchors pride in the real, still-celebrated 1996 Cricket World Cup win —
Sri Lanka's most iconic sporting story — and the Kandyan drum-and-dance
tradition, while deliberately carrying victory phrases in both Sinhala
("Jayawewa") and Tamil ("Vaazhga") to reflect both official languages.
Have native Sinhala and Tamil speakers each review their half before
recording.
