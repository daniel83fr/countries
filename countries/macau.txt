MACAU
Official languages: Chinese (Cantonese), Portuguese

TITLE: "Ga Yau, Macau / Vamos, Macau"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Luso-Cantonese fusion stadium anthem. Portuguese guitarra melody lines
trading with Cantonese gong and cymbal hits, fado-tinged minor-key verses
opening into a firecracker-percussion, brass-driven pop chorus with massed
bilingual crowd vocals — two heritages sharing one small peninsula's roar.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Portuguese guitarra picks a slow line over distant firecrackers)
Macau... ga yau... vamos...

[Verse 1]
Stone facades of old Saint Paul's still standing on the hill,
Portuguese tile and Chinese incense sharing the same still,
Fishing boats and casino lights along the one same bay,
Two languages, one heartbeat, greeting every single day.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the ruins on the hilltop to the harbor down below,
Two worlds became one city — that's the story that we know.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Cantonese + Portuguese + English)
Ga yau, Macau! Vamos, Macau!
(Go, Macau! Come on, Macau!)
Yat chai, Macau! Juntos, Macau!
(Together, Macau! Together, Macau!)

[Verse 2]
Egg tart steam and temple smoke rising the same warm way,
Two flags folded into one on every single game day,
Small peninsula, big story, four centuries deep and true,
When the whistle blows, we cheer it in more than one tongue too.

[Chorus]
Ga yau, Macau! Vamos, Macau!
Yat chai, Macau! Juntos, Macau!

[Bridge]
East met west here long before the world called it that,
We are the bridge, we are the meeting spot —
Two tongues, one roar, echoing round the bay,
This is Macau's anthem for every game day.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Ga yau, Macau! Vamos, Macau!
Yat chai, Macau! Juntos, Macau!
Ga yau! Vamos!

[Outro — chant tag]
Macau! Ga yau! Vamos!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built specifically around Macau's dual Portuguese-Chinese heritage — the
Ruins of St. Paul's, a real landmark fusing both cultures — with the
chorus deliberately carrying both a Cantonese cheer ("ga yau") and a
Portuguese one ("vamos") side by side, since that duality is Macau's
actual identity. Have native Cantonese and Portuguese speakers each review
their half before recording.
