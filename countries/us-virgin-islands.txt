US VIRGIN ISLANDS
Official language: English

TITLE: "Scratch and Roar"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Quelbe-rooted stadium anthem. Quelbe is the US Virgin Islands' own official
territorial music, built on "scratch band" instrumentation — banjo, flute
or squash (a serrated gourd scraper), conga, and the ram's-horn "steel"
triangle — driven here into an up-tempo Carnival J'ouvert groove with a
full-crowd, horn-punctuated chorus.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Squash gourd scrapes in, banjo answers, conga rolls under)
St. Thomas... St. Croix... St. John... scratch and roar...

[Verse 1]
Charlotte Amalie harbor wakes before the sun is high,
Rock City puts her colors on and steps out to the sky,
Emile Griffith threw his hands up from these same warm streets,
This island's always fought for every inch it means to keep.

[Pre-Chorus]
Scratch band tuning up outside the stadium gate,
Every drumbeat says the same three islands, one same fate.

[Chorus]
Scratch and roar! Scratch and roar!
Three islands, one flag, we don't need no more,
Scratch and roar! Scratch and roar!
Virgin Islands rising off that Caribbean shore.

[Verse 2]
J'ouvert morning taught us how to move before we walk,
Every goal that finds the net gets a scratch band talk,
Squash and banjo, steel horn ringing through the heat,
We carry three whole islands in a ninety-minute beat.

[Chorus]
Scratch and roar! Scratch and roar!
Three islands, one flag, we don't need no more,
Scratch and roar! Scratch and roar!
Virgin Islands rising off that Caribbean shore.

[Bridge]
From Magens Bay to Frederiksted's old sugar wall,
Every corner of these islands hears the same call,
Steel horn blowing like the conch shell used to blow,
This is the sound a small place makes when the whole world watch it grow.

[Final Chorus]
Scratch and roar! Scratch and roar!
Virgin Islands rising off that Caribbean shore.

[Outro]
St. Thomas! St. Croix! St. John! Scratch and roar!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Quelbe was declared the official music of the US Virgin Islands, and its
"scratch bands" — built from banjo, squash gourd scrapers, and improvised
percussion — are a real, still-performed folk tradition tied to Carnival
and J'ouvert morning. The lyric also nods to Emile Griffith, the
world-champion boxer born and raised in St. Thomas, as a real symbol of
the territory's fighting pride, and to the three-island unity (St. Thomas,
St. Croix, St. John) that anchors USVI identity.
