CAPE VERDE
Official language: Portuguese (Cape Verdean Creole widely spoken)

TITLE: "Força, Cabo Verde" (Strength, Cape Verde)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Morna-to-coladeira fusion anthem. Cavaquinho and violin carry a morna
melody in the verses — the slow, aching genre Cape Verde gave the world
through Cesária Évora — before the tempo doubles into a coladeira dance
beat for the chorus, with massed crowd vocals and hand-drum accents.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Cavaquinho picks a slow morna figure over a violin sigh)
Cabo Verde... força...

[Verse 1]
Ten volcanic islands scattered on an ocean far from home,
More of us abroad than on the soil where our roots have grown,
Sodade in every morna song, a longing none can name,
But tonight the islands and the diaspora feel the same.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the black-sand shore to a living room across the sea,
One island nation, however far, still beats as we.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Creole/Portuguese + English)
Força, Cabo Verde! Força, Cabo Verde!
(Strength, Cape Verde! Strength, Cape Verde!)
Juntu nos — força, Cabo Verde!
(Together — strength, Cape Verde!)

[Verse 2]
Cesária sang the sodade so the whole world learned to feel,
Now eleven island hearts play for something just as real,
Doesn't matter which time zone is watching on the screen,
Every islander still knows exactly what this means.

[Chorus]
Força, Cabo Verde! Força, Cabo Verde!
Juntu nos — força, Cabo Verde!

[Bridge]
Sodade turns to joy the moment that we score,
Ten small islands, but a diaspora of more —
Cavaquinho slow, then coladeira fast,
This is the anthem built to outlast.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Força, Cabo Verde! Força, Cabo Verde!
Juntu nos — força, Cabo Verde!
Força! Força!

[Outro — chant tag]
Cabo Verde! Força! Força!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on morna (Cape Verde's signature musical genre, made globally famous
by Cesária Évora) and "sodade" (a deep, untranslatable longing central to
Cape Verdean identity), plus the real fact that more Cape Verdeans live
abroad than on the islands themselves — a genuinely distinctive national
story about distance and belonging. Have a native Cape Verdean Creole
speaker review phrasing before recording.
