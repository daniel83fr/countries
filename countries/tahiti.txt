TAHITI
Official languages: French, Tahitian (Reo Tahiti)

TITLE: "Te Pape Nui o te 'Aito" (The Great Roar of the Warrior)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Built on the to'ere — Tahiti's carved wooden slit-drum, the driving
engine of every traditional 'ori Tahiti dance troupe — layered with the
rising, wave-like himene tārava choral style (Tahitian church-derived
group singing, sung in tight rolling harmony) opening into a full-band
stadium chorus sung by the crowd in call-and-response.

LYRICS (Tahitian, with French chorus gloss):

[Fa'a'ohipa]
(To'ere e ta'iri, himene e ho'i mai)
Tahiti... 'aito... 'aito...

[Irava 1]
I Pater te hono nui, te mau'a e ta'iri i te 'āfata,
Te reva o te tuha'a fenua e ha'a i ni'a i te tū'aro,
Te mau tamari'i tāne e tu'u i te fenua i roto i te 'ā'au,
'Aita e mea'i fa'aea, 'aita e mea e 'ore ai.

[Na Mua i te Ho'i]
Mai te miti e taiāha nei i te pae tahatai,
E tae noa atu i te 'āfata e 'ū nei i te reo mo'a.

[Ho'i] — crowd hook: "Tahiti nui, e 'aito e! To'ere e ta'iri, e!" (Great Tahiti, warriors! Let the drum strike!)
Tahiti nui, e 'aito e!
To'ere e ta'iri, e!
Tahiti nui, e 'aito e!
Himene e, himene e!

[Irava 2]
2013, te ao tā'ato'a i hi'o mai i tā mātou nei fenua,
E ere Tahiti i te fenua rii, o te 'ā'au rahi te tāpa'o,
Te reo himene e fa'aitoito ra i te mau tamari'i tū'aro,
'Ori tahi, reo tahi, i raro i te ra'i teie.

[Ho'i]
Tahiti nui, e 'aito e!
To'ere e ta'iri, e!

[Tāpū]
E fa'aroa i te reo himene tārava,
E fa'a'ohipa i te to'ere i te 'āfata,
Te mau matahiapo e hī'o mai mai te pae ha'apa'oraa,
Teie te pese a te fenua, teie te pese a te 'aito.

[Ho'i Hopea]
Tahiti nui, e 'aito e!
To'ere e ta'iri, e!
Tahiti nui, e 'aito e!

[Opani]
Tahiti! Tahiti! E 'aito e!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Centers on Stade Pater Te Hono Nui in Pirae, Tahiti's national football
ground, and the 2013 FIFA Confederations Cup, when Tahiti — a territory
of only around 280,000 people — became the smallest nation ever to
qualify for a FIFA senior tournament, playing Spain, Uruguay and Nigeria
in Brazil. The to'ere slit-drum and himene tārava choral singing are
core, living Tahitian performance traditions, distinct from Hawaiian or
Māori forms. A native Tahitian speaker should review the Reo Tahiti
before recording.
