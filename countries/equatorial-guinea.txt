EQUATORIAL GUINEA
Official languages: Spanish, French, Portuguese

TITLE: "Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Afro-Hispanic fusion stadium anthem. Balélé drum rhythms from Bioko Island
carrying the pulse, Spanish-guitar flamenco-tinged melody lines weaving
through — a genuinely unique blend, since this is the only African nation
with Spanish as an official language — opening into a full-band chorus
with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Balélé drum pattern builds under a Spanish guitar figure)
Guinea Ecuatorial... vamos...

[Verse 1]
An island and a mainland speaking words no neighbor shares,
Spanish verses sung in rhythms only this coast wears,
Fang villages and Bubi drums both calling out as one,
Rainforest and ocean breeze beneath the equator sun.

[Pre-Chorus]
From Bioko's misted mountain to the mainland river's bend,
One small nation, double heartbeat, without an end.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Spanish + English)
Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial! Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial!
(Come on, Equatorial Guinea! Come on, Equatorial Guinea!)
Juntos vamos — Guinea Ecuatorial!
(Together let's go — Equatorial Guinea!)

[Verse 2]
No other nation on this continent will sing it quite this way,
A language crossed an ocean here and chose to stay,
Doesn't matter that the world forgets us on the map,
We built a sound no other corner of the earth can match.

[Chorus]
Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial! Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial!
Juntos vamos — Guinea Ecuatorial!

[Bridge]
Island and mainland, one same flag unfurled,
Speaking a language crossed from another world —
Drum and guitar, ocean and the sun,
This is the anthem of a nation, one.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial! Vamos, Guinea Ecuatorial!
Juntos vamos — Guinea Ecuatorial!
Vamos! Vamos!

[Outro — chant tag]
Guinea Ecuatorial! Vamos!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the genuinely unique fact that Equatorial Guinea is the only
African nation with Spanish as an official language, blended with Bioko
Island's Bubi balélé drumming and mainland Fang culture. Have a native
Equatoguinean Spanish speaker review phrasing before recording.
