NORTHERN IRELAND
Official languages: English (Irish and Ulster Scots also recognized)

TITLE: "Green and White Army"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
A terrace-folk stomp built like a Belfast pub singalong hits the stands —
acoustic guitar and bodhrán driving a four-on-the-floor terrace stomp,
built for thousands of voices bouncing the same two lines back at each
other, exactly the way Windsor Park sings it.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(A bodhrán starts alone, then the whole end of the ground joins in)
Here we go... Green and White Army...

[Verse 1]
Up at the Giant's Causeway the stones stand in their rows,
Down at Windsor Park the same old chant still grows,
Small wee country, big wee heart, that's the story that we tell,
Every match we're the loudest noise that Belfast's ever held.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the shipyard cranes still standing over the Lagan's grey water,
To eleven men in green who carry the whole town after.

[Chorus] — the real, long-used Northern Ireland fan chant
Green and White Army! Green and White Army!
We're not brazil, we're Northern Ireland!
Green and White Army! Green and White Army!
Norn Iron 'til we die!

[Verse 2]
Nineteen eighty-two, the host nation, Spain,
And a boy from Mona called Gerry Armstrong won the game,
Nobody gave us a prayer and we didn't need one either,
That's the same wee spirit every time we're the underdog believer.

[Chorus]
Green and White Army! Green and White Army!
We're not brazil, we're Northern Ireland!

[Bridge]
Causeway stones and shipyard steel, that's the ground we're built upon,
Every away end singing like the whole of home has gone,
Doesn't matter what the odds say written on the wall,
Green and White Army, we'll be here through it all.

[Final Chorus]
Green and White Army! Green and White Army!
We're not brazil, we're Northern Ireland!
Green and White Army! Green and White Army!

[Outro]
Norn Iron! Green and White Army, 'til we die!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built directly on "Green and White Army," the genuine, decades-old
Northern Ireland fan chant heard at every home game, and on the real
1982 World Cup shock win over host nation Spain, scored by Gerry
Armstrong, still the country's proudest football memory. The Giant's
Causeway and Belfast's shipyard cranes (the "Samson and Goliath" over
the River Lagan) are real, specific local landmarks grounding the
underdog pride in the lyric.
