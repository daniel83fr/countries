EGYPT
Official language: Arabic

TITLE: "Ya Misr" (O Egypt)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Mizmar-driven stadium anthem. Mizmar horn and tabla baladi rhythm carrying
the pulse (the sound of a real Cairo street celebration), oud melody
weaving through the verses, opening into a full-band arena chorus with
massed crowd vocals under the shadow of the pyramids.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Mizmar horn wail over a driving tabla baladi rhythm)
Ya Misr... ya Misr...

[Verse 1]
The Nile has carried empires longer than the stars can trace,
Pyramids still watch the sunrise catch a modern city's face,
Every alley in Cairo hums a chant before the game,
Five thousand years of history cheering for the same one name.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the river's ancient current to the stadium's roaring stand,
One nation, oldest story, but the youngest hand.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Arabic + English)
Ya Misr! Ya Misr al-habiba!
(O Egypt! O beloved Egypt!)
Yalla Misr, ya Misr al-habiba!
(Come on Egypt, O beloved Egypt!)

[Verse 2]
The Pharaohs wear the shirt now, not the crown they wore of old,
Every strike still carries stories thousands of years old,
Doesn't matter what the modern scoreboard says tonight,
We are still the river nation that first learned to write.

[Chorus]
Ya Misr! Ya Misr al-habiba!
Yalla Misr, ya Misr al-habiba!

[Bridge]
Stone remembers everyone who's ever come and gone,
The river still keeps flowing and the song goes on —
Mizmar horn and tabla, pyramid and sand,
This is the anthem of the oldest land.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Ya Misr! Ya Misr al-habiba!
Yalla Misr, ya Misr al-habiba!
Ya Misr! Ya Misr!

[Outro — chant tag]
Misr! Misr! Ya habiba!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the Nile and the pyramids — Egypt's defining, globally recognized
landmarks — and "the Pharaohs," the real nickname of Egypt's national
football team, framing modern sporting pride as a continuation of ancient
history rather than a separate story. Have a native Egyptian Arabic
speaker review phrasing before recording.
