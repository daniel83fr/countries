KYRGYZSTAN
Official language: Kyrgyz

TITLE: "Kyrgyzstan, Alga!" (Kyrgyzstan, Forward!)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Nomadic-fusion stadium anthem. Komuz (three-stringed lute) melody over a
galloping horse-hoof rhythm played on frame drums, temir komuz (jaw harp)
texture, building into a full-band chorus with throat-singing-inspired
low harmonies and massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Komuz picking over a slow galloping drum pattern)
Kyrgyzstan... alga...

[Verse 1]
Tian Shan peaks still white above the yurts below the ridge,
The Manas epic longer than a thousand years could bridge,
Horsemen chase the goat across the open mountain field,
Every rider learns that pride is something earned, not sealed.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the yurt door open wide to the stadium's bright glow,
This is a nomad nation that has always known how to go.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Kyrgyz + English)
Kyrgyzstan, alga! Kyrgyzstan, alga!
(Kyrgyzstan, forward! Kyrgyzstan, forward!)
Birge, birge — Kyrgyzstan, alga!
(Together, together — Kyrgyzstan, forward!)

[Verse 2]
Kok-boru riders grip the goat and gallop through the dust,
The whole field roars for horsemen that the mountains taught to trust,
We carry Manas in our chest like an unbroken song,
Eight hundred thousand verses taught us how to carry on.

[Chorus]
Kyrgyzstan, alga! Kyrgyzstan, alga!
Birge, birge — Kyrgyzstan, alga!

[Bridge]
Mountains never asked us to be anything but strong,
The wind across the steppe still carries our song —
Horse and rider, string and drum as one,
This is the nation that outrides the sun.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Kyrgyzstan, alga! Kyrgyzstan, alga!
Birge, birge — Kyrgyzstan, alga!
Alga! Alga!

[Outro — chant tag]
Kyrgyzstan! Alga! Alga!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the Manas epic (one of the world's longest oral epic poems and a
cornerstone of Kyrgyz identity), the Tian Shan mountains, and kok-boru
(the traditional horseback goat-carrying game, a genuine national sporting
passion). "Alga" (forward) is a real, widely understood Kyrgyz/Turkic word
used in exactly this rallying way. Have a native Kyrgyz speaker review
phrasing before recording.
