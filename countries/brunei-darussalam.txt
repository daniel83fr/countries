BRUNEI DARUSSALAM
Official language: Malay (Bahasa Melayu)

TITLE: "Maju Jaya Brunei" (Forward to Glory, Brunei)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Malay zapin-fusion stadium anthem. Gulingtangan gong-chime ensemble and
rebana frame drums driving the groove, gambus lute picking out the melody
line, building into a full-band pop-rock chorus with massed crowd vocals —
traditional Bruneian court music meeting a modern football terrace.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Gulingtangan gongs ring out over a slow rebana pulse)
Brunei... Darussalam...

[Verse 1]
On stilts above the water, Kampong Ayer wakes at dawn,
Golden domes catch the sunrise before the day moves on,
A small nation by the sea, quiet but never small in heart,
When the whistle blows, this whole river plays its part.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the water village to the mosque's golden crown,
We carry this small nation's pride from house to house to town.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Malay + English)
Brunei Darussalam, maju jaya!
(Brunei Darussalam, forward to glory!)
Brunei Darussalam, kita jaya!
(Brunei Darussalam, we are victorious!)

[Verse 2]
The gongs still ring the way they rang a hundred years before,
Every family on the water bangs a drum against the floor,
We don't need to be the biggest name that they all know,
We just need eleven hearts to make the river glow.

[Chorus]
Brunei Darussalam, maju jaya!
Brunei Darussalam, kita jaya!

[Bridge]
Stilts and gold and quiet, patient pride,
The smallest nations still can turn the tide —
One flag, one Sultan's blessing on us all,
Together standing, together we won't fall.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Brunei Darussalam, maju jaya!
Brunei Darussalam, kita jaya!
Maju jaya! Maju jaya!

[Outro — chant tag]
Brunei! Brunei! Maju jaya!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Anchors the pride in Kampong Ayer, the historic stilt water village that is
genuinely one of Brunei's most distinctive landmarks, and the gulingtangan
gong ensemble, a real traditional Bruneian instrument. "Maju Jaya" (forward
to glory/prosper) directly echoes language used in Brunei's own national
motto and civic rhetoric, so it will land as authentic rather than
generic. Have a native Brunei Malay speaker review phrasing before
recording.
