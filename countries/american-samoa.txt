AMERICAN SAMOA
Official languages: Samoan, English

TITLE: "Nicky's Net"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Samoan gospel-choir stadium anthem. Deep, layered a cappella-style choir
harmonies (the backbone of Samoan church and rugby-sideline singing) build
over fa'ataupati slap-dance body percussion and a log-drum (pate) pulse,
opening into a full-throated massed chant.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Pate log drum knocks a slow heartbeat, choir hums low)
Amerika Samoa... tatou o se atunuu... fa'afetai...

[Verse 1]
Thirty-one to nothing, that scoreline stung for years,
Nicky Salapu stood right there and swallowed all the tears,
Same man, same gloves, same island, waited eight long years to stand,
Two-one over Tonga, and the ball was in his hands.

[Pre-Chorus]
Tafuna's small green field held a whole nation's breath,
Fa'a Samoa carried us past every kind of test.

[Chorus]
Amerika Samoa! Talofa, rise!
One small island, one big roaring cry,
Amerika Samoa! Talofa, rise!
We waited, we believed, and we did not compromise.

[Verse 2]
Slap-dance rhythm on the sideline, choir behind the goal,
Every siva step out here is stitched into our soul,
Fa'afetai for the coach who saw what we could be,
Ua manuia — we are blessed — from mountain to the sea.

[Chorus]
Amerika Samoa! Talofa, rise!
One small island, one big roaring cry,
Amerika Samoa! Talofa, rise!
We waited, we believed, and we did not compromise.

[Bridge]
Pate drum, slapping hands, voices climbing high,
The whole of Pago Pago singing at the sky,
Eight years of losing built the joy in this one win,
Fa'a Samoa taught us how a small place still gets in.

[Final Chorus]
Amerika Samoa! Talofa, rise!
Amerika Samoa, we did not compromise.

[Outro]
Talofa! Amerika Samoa! Manuia!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the real story of goalkeeper Nicky Salapu, who conceded 31 goals
in American Samoa's infamous 2001 loss to Australia and, still in goal a
decade later, kept a clean sheet as they beat Tonga 2-1 in 2011 for their
first-ever official win — a story later told in the documentary "Next
Goal Wins." The lyric leans on "fa'a Samoa" (the Samoan way) and Samoan
choral and fa'ataupati slap-dance traditions central to island identity.
