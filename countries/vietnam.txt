VIETNAM
Official language: Vietnamese

TITLE: "Việt Nam, Cố Lên!" (Vietnam, Go!)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Đàn bầu-driven stadium anthem. Đàn bầu (Vietnam's unique one-string
monochord) carries the wavering melodic hook over a trống cơm hand-drum
rhythm, water-puppet-theater percussion accents, opening into a full-band
arena chorus with massed crowd vocals — a motorbike-horn city energy
folded into the beat.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Đàn bầu bends a single note over a slow trống cơm pulse)
Việt Nam... cố lên...

[Verse 1]
Red river delta, Mekong delta, cradling the same one land,
Conical hats bent low in rice fields, every worn-down hand,
Water puppets dancing on a pond that's been a stage for years,
Every goal we've ever scored has silenced someone's fears.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the limestone karsts of Ha Long to the scooter-crowded street,
One nation, north to south, moving on one beat.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Vietnamese + English — a real, iconic national cheer)
Việt Nam, cố lên! Việt Nam, cố lên!
(Vietnam, go! Vietnam, go!)
Tất cả cùng nhau — Việt Nam, cố lên!
(All together — Vietnam, go!)

[Verse 2]
A grandmother on a plastic stool beside a noodle stand,
Watching the same match as a worker on a foreign land,
Doesn't matter where you're standing when the whole match starts to sway,
Every heart in every city beats the same on game day.

[Chorus]
Việt Nam, cố lên! Việt Nam, cố lên!
Tất cả cùng nhau — Việt Nam, cố lên!

[Bridge]
One string can carry every feeling that we've got,
One nation, bent but never broken by the shot —
Delta and mountain, water and the sun,
This is the anthem of a people not undone.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Việt Nam, cố lên! Việt Nam, cố lên!
Tất cả cùng nhau — Việt Nam, cố lên!
Cố lên! Cố lên!

[Outro — chant tag]
Việt Nam! Cố lên!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on "cố lên" (literally "try harder/go on"), the single most iconic
and universally used Vietnamese cheer at every sporting event, paired with
the đàn bầu — a genuinely unique Vietnamese instrument capable of
bending a single string into a full melody. Have a native Vietnamese
speaker confirm tonal accuracy before recording, since Vietnamese is tonal
and melody must respect word tones.
