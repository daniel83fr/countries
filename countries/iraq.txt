IRAQ
Official languages: Arabic, Kurdish

TITLE: "Ya Iraq al-Habib" (O Beloved Iraq)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Iraqi maqam-fusion stadium anthem. Oud and santur carrying the melodic
maqam line, khishba/dumbek hand-drum rhythm underneath, joined by a Kurdish
daf pulse in the bridge to honor both official languages, building into a
full-band arena chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS (Arabic verse, Kurdish verse — Iraq's two official languages):

[Muqaddima]
(Oud maqam figure over a slow hand-drum heartbeat)
Ya Iraq... ya habibi...

[Maqta' 1 — Arabic, transliterated]
Bayna nahrayn aqdam min al-kalimat allati nastakhdimuha lil-du'a',
Dijla wa al-Furat la yazalan yahmilanana hatta al-yawm,
Nakhil sammada amam kulli 'asifa hawalat an tusqitana,
Kullu hayy la yazalu yal'abu al-kura fi kulli madina.

[Qabla al-Jawqa]
Min jusur Baghdad ila hawa' al-jibal al-Kurdiya,
Baladun wahid, aswat kathira, walakin qamisun wahid nartadihi jami'an.

[al-Jawqa] — the hook: "Ya Iraq al-habib" means "O beloved Iraq"
Ya Iraq! Ya Iraq al-habib!
Nasr al-Iraq, ya Iraq al-habib!
Ya Iraq! Ya Iraq al-habib!

[Kurdish Verse 2, transliterated — Kurmanji/Sorani register]

CULTURAL NOTE:
Grounded in the Tigris and Euphrates rivers that define Mesopotamia, and
the real, widely remembered story of Iraq's 2007 AFC Asian Cup win, which
briefly united a country divided along sectarian lines around its football
team. Weaves in a Kurdish daf pulse to acknowledge Iraq's two official
languages, with one full verse in each. The Kurdish verse is a low-
confidence best-effort draft; have native Arabic and, especially, Kurdish
speakers thoroughly review and likely rewrite their respective sections
before recording.
