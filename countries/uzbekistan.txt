UZBEKISTAN
Official language: Uzbek

TITLE: "O'zbekiston, Oldinga!" (Uzbekistan, Forward!)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Silk Road-fusion stadium anthem. Dutar and rubab lute melody lines over a
doira frame-drum rhythm, maqom-style vocal ornamentation in the verses,
opening into a full-band arena chorus with massed crowd vocals echoing
the tiled domes of Samarkand.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Dutar figure over a slow doira frame-drum pulse)
O'zbekiston... oldinga...

[Verse 1]
Registan's tiles still catch the sun the way they did before,
Caravans once carried silk through here from shore to distant shore,
Every family's table holds a plov pot passed by hand,
Every wrestler's grip still honors how this land was planned.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the turquoise dome at sunrise to the cotton field below,
One nation, ancient crossroads, carrying its glow.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Uzbek + English)
O'zbekiston, oldinga! O'zbekiston, oldinga!
(Uzbekistan, forward! Uzbekistan, forward!)
Birga, birga — O'zbekiston, oldinga!
(Together, together — Uzbekistan, forward!)

[Verse 2]
Kurash wrestlers grip the belt the way their fathers always did,
Every empire passed through here, but we're the ones who stayed,
When the whole stand rises for the whistle and the run,
We carry the same crossroads pride that empires never won.

[Chorus]
O'zbekiston, oldinga! O'zbekiston, oldinga!
Birga, birga — O'zbekiston, oldinga!

[Bridge]
Silk Road dust still settles on the same old stone,
Every caravan that passed here left us more than gone —
Dutar string and drumbeat, tile and dome,
This is the anthem of a crossroads that calls this home.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
O'zbekiston, oldinga! O'zbekiston, oldinga!
Birga, birga — O'zbekiston, oldinga!
Oldinga! Oldinga!

[Outro — chant tag]
O'zbekiston! Oldinga!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on Samarkand's Registan and the Silk Road heritage that made
Uzbekistan a genuine historical crossroads, plov (the national communal
dish, deeply tied to hospitality and celebration), and kurash (traditional
Uzbek belt wrestling). "Oldinga" (forward) is a real Uzbek word. Have a
native Uzbek speaker review phrasing before recording.
