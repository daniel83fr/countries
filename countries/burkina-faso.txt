BURKINA FASO
Official language: French (Mooré widely spoken)

TITLE: "Les Hommes Intègres" (The Upright People)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Balafon-driven stadium anthem. Balafon (West African wooden xylophone)
carrying the melodic hook over djembe and talking-drum polyrhythms,
building into a full-band Afro-pop chorus with massed crowd vocals — the
sound of FESPACO's opening parade turned into a match-day anthem.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Balafon runs cascade over a rising djembe pattern)
Burkina... Burkina Faso...

[Verse 1]
Our name itself means upright people, land of honest hands,
Balafon keys have carried honor across these ochre lands,
Masks dance in the Bwa villages under a burning sky,
And every generation learns exactly how, and why.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the balafon's wooden keys to the stadium's open door,
One nation, name and honor, carrying so much more.

[Chorus] (viral hook, French + English)
Burkina Faso, en avant! Burkina Faso, en avant!
(Burkina Faso, forward! Burkina Faso, forward!)
Les hommes intègres, en avant!
(The upright people, forward!)

[Verse 2]
The Stallions charge the pitch the way our name demands we stand,
Honest, upright, never bending to the pressure of the land,
Doesn't matter what the scoreboard or the map's edge shows,
We are the country whose own name is the pride it grows.

[Chorus]
Burkina Faso, en avant! Burkina Faso, en avant!
Les hommes intègres, en avant!

[Bridge]
A name is not just letters, it's a promise that we keep,
Upright when it's easy, upright when the hill is steep —
Balafon key and drumbeat, mask and the sun,
This is the anthem of the honest ones.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Burkina Faso, en avant! Burkina Faso, en avant!
Les hommes intègres, en avant!
En avant! En avant!

[Outro — chant tag]
Burkina Faso! En avant!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built directly on the country's own name — "Burkina Faso" literally means
"Land of Upright/Honest People," combining Mooré and Dioula — making
national character the actual etymology of the hook rather than an
invented slogan. The Stallions ("Les Étalons") is the national football
team's real nickname. Have a native French/Mooré speaker review phrasing
before recording.
