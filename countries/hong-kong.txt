HONG KONG
Official languages: Chinese (Cantonese), English

TITLE: "Heung Gong Ga Yau" (Go, Hong Kong)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Cantopop stadium anthem with dragon-boat drumming at its core. A massive
dragon-boat drum sets the pulse (the actual paced heartbeat rowers race to),
gongs and cymbals from lion dance tradition punctuating the verses, synths
and electric guitar lifting the chorus into full Cantopop-arena scale.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Dragon-boat drum beats a steady racing pulse)
Heung Gong... ga yau...

[Verse 1]
Neon rivers running down between the mountain and the bay,
A hundred paddles hitting water in the same tight sway,
Victoria Harbour holds the lights of a million different dreams,
Small on the map but louder than a city twice its size seems.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the peak above the skyline to the harbour down below,
This city rows together, this city finds its flow.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Cantonese + English)
Heung Gong, ga yau! Heung Gong, ga yau!
(Hong Kong, go! Hong Kong, go!)
Yat chai ga yau, Heung Gong ga yau!
(Everyone together, go Hong Kong!)

[Verse 2]
The drummer at the bow keeps time for every straining arm,
No storm has ever sunk the pride that keeps this harbour warm,
Underdogs before the whistle, still we take the field,
This city never learned the shape of standing down or kneeled.

[Chorus]
Heung Gong, ga yau! Heung Gong, ga yau!
Yat chai ga yau, Heung Gong ga yau!

[Bridge]
Every paddle stroke is a heartbeat we all share,
Every drumbeat says that we are always there —
Skyscraper shadows, harbour lights below,
This is the rhythm only Hong Kong knows.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Heung Gong, ga yau! Heung Gong, ga yau!
Yat chai ga yau, Heung Gong ga yau!
Ga yau! Ga yau!

[Outro — chant tag]
Ga yau! Ga yau! Heung Gong!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Centers on dragon boat racing, one of Hong Kong's most globally recognized
and genuinely home-grown sports traditions, alongside Victoria Harbour's
iconic skyline. "Ga yau" (加油) is the Cantonese reading of the same cheer
used across the Chinese-speaking world, written here specifically as
Hongkongers say it. Have a native Cantonese speaker confirm tone and
phrasing before recording.
