COMOROS
Official languages: Comorian (Shikomori), Arabic, French

TITLE: "Allez, Comores" (Go, Comoros)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Twarab-fusion island anthem. Oud and violin carry a twarab melody (the
Swahili-coast musical tradition Comoros shares with Zanzibar) over a
hand-drum island rhythm, ylang-ylang market ambience texture, opening into
a full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Oud figure over a slow hand-drum pulse, ocean wind)
Comores... allez, Comores...

[Verse 1]
Karthala's volcano breathes above the ylang-ylang trees,
Perfume drifting off these islands out across the Indian seas,
A fish thought lost to time was found still swimming near our shore,
Proof this small nation holds more wonder than the world's aware of, and more.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the volcano's smoking crest to the harbor's gentle sway,
Three small islands, one same rhythm, carrying the day.

[Chorus] (viral hook, French/Arabic + English)
Allez, Comores! Allez, Comores!
(Go, Comoros! Go, Comoros!)
Ensemble, yalla — allez, Comores!
(Together, come on — go, Comoros!)

[Verse 2]
The Coelacanths swim quiet in the deep the world forgot,
Now eleven island players prove that quiet holds a lot,
Doesn't matter that we're small against the map's wide frame,
An island this size still knows how to carry a name.

[Chorus]
Allez, Comores! Allez, Comores!
Ensemble, yalla — allez, Comores!

[Bridge]
Volcano smoke and perfume in the air,
A living fossil proving we were always there —
Three islands rising from the same deep blue,
This is the anthem of a nation coming through.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Allez, Comores! Allez, Comores!
Ensemble, yalla — allez, Comores!
Allez! Allez!

[Outro — chant tag]
Comores! Allez! Allez!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on ylang-ylang (Comoros is one of the world's largest producers, and
its scent is genuinely part of daily life), Mount Karthala (an active
volcano), and the coelacanth — a prehistoric fish believed extinct until
found alive near the Comoros, which is also the real nickname of the
national football team. Have native Shikomori/Arabic/French speakers
review phrasing before recording.
