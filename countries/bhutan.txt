BHUTAN
Official language: Dzongkha

TITLE: "Druk Yul" (Land of the Thunder Dragon)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Bhutanese dranyen-driven anthem. Dranyen (Bhutanese lute) and lingm (flute)
melody over a slow, deep dungchen horn drone, building through a monastic
call-and-response into a driving frame-drum stadium beat for the choruses —
the sound of an archery-ground crowd singing between shots turned into a
full anthem.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Long dungchen horn note fades under dranyen picking)
Druk Yul... Druk Yul...

[Verse 1]
High where the prayer flags snap in the mountain wind,
The archers draw their bows and the whole village grins,
They sing before the arrow, they dance before the shot,
Happiness is measured here by something gold can't buy or plot.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the dzong on the ridge to the valley below,
One dragon, one kingdom, one heartbeat, one glow.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Dzongkha + English)
Druk Yul! Druk Yul!
(Land of the Thunder Dragon!)
Druk Tsendhen, Druk Yul!
(Our peaceful Thunder Dragon land!)

[Verse 2]
No trophy shines as bright as a target truly found,
When the arrow lands the whole team turns and dances round,
We didn't build our pride on how tall the towers grow,
We built it on the mountain and the wind and what we know.

[Chorus]
Druk Yul! Druk Yul!
Druk Tsendhen, Druk Yul!

[Bridge]
Count us not in medals but in mornings full of peace,
The thunder in the dragon never has to cease —
Small kingdom, quiet kingdom, steady as the snow,
Watch us draw the bowstring and watch the arrow go.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Druk Yul! Druk Yul!
Druk Tsendhen, Druk Yul!
Druk Yul! Druk Yul!

[Outro — chant tag]
Druk Yul! Druk Yul! Druk Yul!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Centers on Bhutan's actual national sport, archery (datse), where teams
famously sing and dance to celebrate — and sometimes to psych out the other
side — after every shot, plus the country's global reputation for
prioritizing Gross National Happiness over GDP. "Druk Yul" (Land of the
Thunder Dragon) is Bhutan's own name for itself in Dzongkha and is
instantly recognizable to Bhutanese people, making it a natural, authentic
chant. Have a native Dzongkha speaker confirm the verse phrasing before
recording.
