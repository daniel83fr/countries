PALESTINE
Official language: Arabic

TITLE: "Ya Falastin" (O Palestine)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Levantine dabke-driven anthem. Oud and mijwiz reed pipe carrying the
melody over a derbake hand-drum stomp built for dabke line-dancing,
embroidery-inspired call-and-response vocal patterns, opening into a
full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Mijwiz reed pipe line over a slow derbake pulse)
Ya Falastin... ya Falastin...

[Verse 1]
Olive trees still standing older than the roads that pass them by,
Tatreez stitched in every thread beneath an open sky,
Grandmother's hands still carry patterns passed from hand to hand,
Every embroidered flower tells the story of this land.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the olive grove at dawn to the dabke line at night,
This is a people carrying their story into the light.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Arabic + English)
Ya Falastin! Ya Falastin al-habib!
(O Palestine! O beloved Palestine!)
Yalla Falastin, ya Falastin al-habib!
(Come on Palestine, O beloved Palestine!)

[Verse 2]
Shoulder locked to shoulder in the dabke's steady stride,
Eleven men in green and white still carry the same pride,
When the whistle blows, the whole diaspora leans in tight,
One team, one story, playing under one same light.

[Chorus]
Ya Falastin! Ya Falastin al-habib!
Yalla Falastin, ya Falastin al-habib!

[Bridge]
The olive tree bends but its roots never leave,
Every embroidered thread is a story we believe —
Hand in hand, foot to foot, we still stand,
This is the song of an unbroken land.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Ya Falastin! Ya Falastin al-habib!
Yalla Falastin, ya Falastin al-habib!
Ya Falastin! Ya Falastin!

[Outro — chant tag]
Falastin! Falastin! Ya habib!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on olive trees (a deep, widely recognized symbol of Palestinian
identity and rootedness) and tatreez, the traditional embroidery whose
patterns carry village and family history, plus dabke's shoulder-to-
shoulder dance formation as a metaphor for unity. Kept deliberately focused
on culture and sport rather than politics. Have a native Palestinian Arabic
speaker review phrasing before recording.
