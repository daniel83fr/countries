TIMOR-LESTE
Official languages: Tetum, Portuguese

TITLE: "Timor Lorosa'e" (Land of the Rising Sun)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Tetum-Portuguese fusion anthem for a young nation. Dadolek bamboo
percussion and babadok hand-drum rhythm carrying the pulse, Portuguese
guitarra melody woven through the verses, opening into a full-band chorus
with massed crowd vocals — the sound of tais cloth patterns turned into
music.

LYRICS (Tetum verse, Portuguese verse — Timor-Leste's two official languages):

[Intro]
(Babadok drum pulse rises under a bamboo dadolek pattern)
Timor Lorosa'e... Timor Lorosa'e...

[Versu 1 — Tetum]
Foho sira kaptura naroman uluk liu antes mundu seluk hadeer,
Kafé kuda iha lakan-badak ba fulan-fulan produsaun ba atus tinan,
Tais tain hosi liña ida-idak ho istoria ida ke ema seidauk hasees,
Nasaun foun ida moris husi luta, aprende atu kura an.

[Pré-Refrão — Português]
Da crista da montanha ao amanhecer até a maré da manhã no porto,
Uma pequena nação, recém-erguida, carregando o seu orgulho, o seu norte.

[Refrén / Refrão] — the hook: "Timor Lorosa'e" means "Land of the Rising Sun"; "ita nia rai" means "this is our land"
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!
Timor Lorosa'e!

[Versu 2 — Tetum]
Fiu ida-idak husi tais ida-idak konta istoria ne'ebé la mate,
Foho-foho aldeia hotu prova katak nasaun ida ne'e kontinua moris,
Bainhira apitu soa, bandeira foun sa'e hamutuk ho loro-matan,
Ida ne'e mak nasaun ida seidauk hotu hakerek nia istoria.

[Refrén]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!

[Ponte — Português]
O sol nasce primeiro aqui antes de qualquer lugar perto,
A manhã de uma jovem nação, orgulhosa e desperta —
Fio tecido e montanha, café e a maré que retorna,
Este é o hino do orgulho de uma nação que se adorna.

[Refrén Final]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!

[Rematadura]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on "Timor Lorosa'e," Timor-Leste's own Tetum name for itself ("Land
of the Rising Sun," referencing the sunrise seen from its eastern tip), and
tais, the traditional hand-woven cloth whose patterns carry family and
regional identity. Frames the country's youth as a story since independence
rather than generic pride. Written with one full Tetum verse and one full
Portuguese verse to represent both official languages; have native Tetum
and Portuguese speakers each review their half before recording.
