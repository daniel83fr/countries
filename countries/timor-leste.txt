TIMOR-LESTE
Official languages: Tetum, Portuguese

TITLE: "Timor Lorosa'e" (Land of the Rising Sun)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Tetum-Portuguese fusion anthem for a young nation. Dadolek bamboo
percussion and babadok hand-drum rhythm carrying the pulse, Portuguese
guitarra melody woven through the verses, opening into a full-band chorus
with massed crowd vocals — the sound of tais cloth patterns turned into
music.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Babadok drum pulse rises under a bamboo dadolek pattern)
Timor Lorosa'e... Timor Lorosa'e...

[Verse 1]
Mountains catch the first light before the rest of the world wakes,
Coffee grown on terraced slopes for a hundred harvests' sake,
Tais cloth woven thread by thread with stories none can steal,
A young nation born from struggle learning how to heal.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the mountain ridge at sunrise to the harbor's morning tide,
One small nation, newly standing, carrying its pride.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Tetum + English)
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
(Land of the rising sun! Land of the rising sun!)
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!
(This is our land, land of the rising sun!)

[Verse 2]
Every thread of every tais tells a story that survived,
Every mountain village proved this nation stayed alive,
When the whistle blows the young flag rises with the sun,
This is a country still writing the story it's begun.

[Chorus]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!

[Bridge]
The sun rises first here before anywhere near,
A young nation's morning, proud and clear —
Woven thread and mountain, coffee and the tide,
This is the anthem of a nation's rising pride.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!
Ita nia rai, Timor Lorosa'e!

[Outro — chant tag]
Timor Lorosa'e! Timor Lorosa'e!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on "Timor Lorosa'e," Timor-Leste's own Tetum name for itself ("Land
of the Rising Sun," referencing the sunrise seen from its eastern tip), and
tais, the traditional hand-woven cloth whose patterns carry family and
regional identity. Frames the country's youth as a story since independence
rather than generic pride. Have a native Tetum speaker review phrasing
before recording.
