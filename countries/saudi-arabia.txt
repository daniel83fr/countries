SAUDI ARABIA
Official language: Arabic

TITLE: "Ya Saudia" (O Saudi)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Ardah-driven stadium anthem. Ardah sword-dance drums and chant patterns
(the real Najdi celebration tradition — lines of men, swords raised, poetry
chanted over drums) form the backbone, oud melody threading through the
verses, opening into a full-band arena chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Ardah drum pattern builds, poetry-chant murmur underneath)
Ya Saudia... ya Saudia...

[Verse 1]
Swords raised high in a line as old as the desert's memory,
Poets chanting verses no army could ever silence or bury,
Falcons circling high above the dunes at break of day,
And a football pitch that turned a giant's certainty to clay.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the Najdi drum line to the roaring stadium crowd,
One nation, ancient and modern, singing out loud.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Arabic + English)
Ya Saudia! Ya Saudia al-habiba!
(O Saudi! O beloved Saudi!)
Yalla Saudia, ya Saudia al-habiba!
(Come on Saudi, O beloved Saudi!)

[Verse 2]
They said the underdogs don't topple giants built that tall,
Then eleven green shirts proved the whole world wrong with one football,
The ardah drums that celebrated victories long before,
Beat the same rhythm when we walked off as the ones who scored.

[Chorus]
Ya Saudia! Ya Saudia al-habiba!
Yalla Saudia, ya Saudia al-habiba!

[Bridge]
Swords don't fall in the ardah, they rise as one,
Every drumbeat says the celebration's just begun —
Desert and falcon, poem and blade,
This is the pride that centuries have made.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Ya Saudia! Ya Saudia al-habiba!
Yalla Saudia, ya Saudia al-habiba!
Ya Saudia! Ya Saudia!

[Outro — chant tag]
Saudia! Saudia! Ya habiba!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the ardah, Saudi Arabia's traditional sword-dance and poetry
performance genuinely used at national celebrations, and the country's
famous 2022 World Cup upset victory over a defending champion, a real,
still-celebrated underdog moment. Have a native Saudi Arabic speaker review
phrasing before recording.
