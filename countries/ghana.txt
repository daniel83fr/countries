GHANA
Official language: English (Twi widely spoken)

TITLE: "Ayekoo, Black Stars" (Well Done, Black Stars)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Highlife-driven stadium anthem. Highlife guitar lines (the genre Ghana
gave to all of West Africa) over a fontomfrom talking-drum rhythm, kente
loom-clack percussion texture, opening into a full-band horn-driven chorus
with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Talking drum "speaks" over a rising highlife guitar riff)
Ghana... ayekoo...

[Verse 1]
Kente thread is woven with a meaning in each line,
Every color, every pattern tells a story, tells a sign,
Gold once brought the whole world sailing to this very coast,
Now the Black Star on the flag is what we're proud of most.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the loom that weaves the kente to the stadium's roaring floor,
One nation, Black Star rising, carrying more.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Twi + English — a real, widely used Akan word)
Ayekoo, Black Stars! Ayekoo, Black Stars!
(Well done, Black Stars! Well done, Black Stars!)
Yɛn ara, ayekoo — Black Stars!
(This is ours, well done — Black Stars!)

[Verse 2]
The highlife horns still play the way our grandparents danced them slow,
Now the tempo's doubled but the melody's the same old glow,
Doesn't matter what the years or scoreboards say we've lost,
Every thread of kente teaches what real patience costs.

[Chorus]
Ayekoo, Black Stars! Ayekoo, Black Stars!
Yɛn ara, ayekoo — Black Stars!

[Bridge]
Every pattern in the cloth has a name and a claim,
Every drumbeat in this nation carries the same flame —
Talking drum and highlife horn as one,
This is the anthem underneath the sun.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Ayekoo, Black Stars! Ayekoo, Black Stars!
Yɛn ara, ayekoo — Black Stars!
Ayekoo! Ayekoo!

[Outro — chant tag]
Ghana! Ayekoo! Black Stars!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on kente cloth (whose patterns each carry real, specific meanings in
Akan culture), highlife music (Ghana's own genre, exported across West
Africa), and "the Black Stars," the beloved real nickname of Ghana's
national football team. "Ayekoo" is a genuine Twi/Akan expression of
congratulation. Have a native Twi speaker review phrasing before recording.
