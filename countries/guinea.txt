GUINEA
Official language: French

TITLE: "Allez, Syli" (Go, Elephant)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Djembe-driven stadium anthem. Djembe and dunun drum ensemble carrying the
full rhythmic backbone — Guinea sits at the heart of the djembe's West
African birthplace — kora melody threading through, opening into a
full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(A single djembe slap answered by a rising dunun bass pattern)
Guinée... allez, Syli...

[Verse 1]
Fouta Djallon's highlands feed three rivers to the sea,
Every djembe ever carved learned its first beat here, from a tree,
The Syli elephant walks slow but never walks away,
Every match-day drum circle carries what the elders say.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the highland source of rivers to the stadium's roaring crowd,
One nation, rhythm-rooted, drumming proud.

[Chorus] (viral hook, French + English)
Allez, Syli! Allez, Guinée!
(Go, Elephant! Go, Guinea!)
Ensemble on frappe — allez, Guinée!
(Together we strike — go, Guinea!)

[Verse 2]
The dunun bass still anchors every dancer's steady feet,
Every djembe solo answers with a rhythm hard to beat,
Doesn't matter what the smaller nations think we've got,
The elephant remembers every single thing, forgot nothing not.

[Chorus]
Allez, Syli! Allez, Guinée!
Ensemble on frappe — allez, Guinée!

[Bridge]
Djembe hand and river source, one and the same,
Every rhythm in this land answers to the name —
Highland water feeding every drum,
This is the anthem of where the beat's from.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Allez, Syli! Allez, Guinée!
Ensemble on frappe — allez, Guinée!
Allez! Allez!

[Outro — chant tag]
Guinée! Allez! Syli!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on the djembe drum, whose origins trace directly to Guinea's Mandé
region, and "Syli" (meaning "elephant" in Susu), the real nickname of
Guinea's national football team. The Fouta Djallon highlands are the
genuine source of several major West African rivers. Have a native French
speaker review phrasing before recording.
