FIJI
Official languages: English, Fijian, Fiji Hindi

TITLE: "Lali Calling"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Meke and vude-rooted stadium anthem. The lali — a hollowed wooden slit
drum traditionally struck to summon a village — opens and drives the
track, with vude's dance-pop guitar groove and massed meke-style
call-and-response vocals building into the chorus.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Lali drum sounds the old calling rhythm, guitar answers in vude style)
Viti... au sa qai lai... lali e tagi...

[Verse 1]
Suva harbor hears the lali long before the kickoff comes,
Every village on these islands answers when that wood drum drums,
Kava bowl still passing round while the team walks out the tunnel,
Same old calling, older than the stadium's every channel.

[Pre-Chorus]
Meke dancers stamping time the way our strikers stamp the ground,
One sound calling every island, one deep wooden sound.

[Chorus]
Viti! Viti! Lali e tagi!
The drum is calling, and the whole coast hears it ring,
Viti! Viti! Lali e tagi!
Bula vinaka — rise up, Fiji, sing!

[Verse 2]
Fiji Hindi and the Fijian tongue both roar the same loud cheer,
Sugarcane and coral reef both send their sons out here,
Vude guitar keeps the whole crowd swaying left to right,
Lali drum and stadium lights turn evening into fight.

[Chorus]
Viti! Viti! Lali e tagi!
The drum is calling, and the whole coast hears it ring,
Viti! Viti! Lali e tagi!
Bula vinaka — rise up, Fiji, sing!

[Bridge]
Lali struck the warning once, now it strikes the joy,
Every island sending out its daughter and its boy,
Vinaka to the ancestors who taught this drum to speak,
We are still one calling nation, strong from peak to peak.

[Final Chorus]
Viti! Viti! Lali e tagi!
Bula vinaka — rise up, Fiji, sing!

[Outro]
Lali e tagi! Viti! Bula!

CULTURAL NOTE:
The lali is a real, still-used wooden slit drum traditionally sounded
across Fijian villages to call meetings, mark events, or summon people
together — here repurposed as the sound that calls the nation to a match.
"Bula vinaka" is Fiji's genuine everyday greeting, and the lyric
deliberately holds both Fijian and Fiji Hindi speakers together, since
Fiji's football-supporting public spans both communities.
