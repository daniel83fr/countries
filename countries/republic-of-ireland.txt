REPUBLIC OF IRELAND
Official languages: Irish (Gaeilge), English

TITLE: "Boys in Green"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Irish trad session driven straight into a stadium rock stomp. Bodhrán
frame drum sets the marching pulse, uilleann pipes and fiddle trade the
melody over a full-band build, and the whole thing resolves into a
massed, pub-session style crowd singalong — verses carried by a lead
voice, chorus roared back by thousands, exactly how the terraces already
sing.

LYRICS (Irish verse, English verse — Ireland's two official languages):

[Intro]
(Bodhrán alone, then uilleann pipes rise underneath)
Suas leis na Buachaillí Glasa...

[Véarsa 1 — Gaeilge]
Ó Dhalymount go dtí an Aviva, an seanamhrán céanna a chanaimid,
Gach contae ag seoladh a chroí chun seasamh taobh thiar den ghlas,
An fhidil is an bodhrán ag bualadh mar a d'aithin ár n-aithreacha,
Sé mo laoch, mo ghile mear — níl an bród seo nua ar chor ar bith.

[Réamhchurfá]
Tá muid linn go deo, tríd an ngaoth is tríd an mbáisteach,
Leanaimid gach cluiche, bua nó cailliúint, arís is arís eile.

[Curfá] — terrace-style call and response
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Suas leis na Buachaillí Glasa, canfaimid é oíche is lá!
Éire! Éire!
Ní shárófadh Tír na nÓg an glas a chaithimid inniu!

[Verse 2 — English]
No matter what the scoreboard says when the final whistle blows,
The Boys in Green walk off the pitch to a roar that always grows,
From Cork to Donegal, from Dublin to Mayo,
Is cuma cén stáisiún — one song, everywhere we go.

[Curfá]
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Suas leis na Buachaillí Glasa, canfaimid é oíche is lá!
Éire! Éire!
Ní shárófadh Tír na nÓg an glas a chaithimid inniu!

[Droichead]
Tabhair dom geansaí is seisiún faoi lán seoil,
Na píoba, an druma, an seastán ar fad ina seasamh ag canadh,
Amhrán ár muintire, curtha ar aghaidh síos an líne,
Níos mó ná peil atá anseo — seo am na hÉireann.

[Curfá Deiridh]
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Suas leis na Buachaillí Glasa — a Éire, éist linn ag rá!

[Deireadh]
Suas leis! Up the Boys in Green!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built around "The Boys in Green," the real, affectionate nickname for the
Republic of Ireland national football team, and the terrace chant "Ólé
Ólé Ólé," a genuine fixture of Irish away support since the Italia '90
era. Written with a full Irish-language verse and a full English verse to
represent both of Ireland's official languages substantively, plus
Irish-language phrases in the chorus and bridge that already circulate in
real Irish sporting culture. The style draws on Irish trad session music
(bodhrán, uilleann pipes, fiddle). Have a native Irish speaker review the
Gaeilge verse's grammar and idiom before recording.
