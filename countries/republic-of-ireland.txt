REPUBLIC OF IRELAND
Official languages: Irish (Gaeilge), English

TITLE: "Boys in Green"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Irish trad session driven straight into a stadium rock stomp. Bodhrán
frame drum sets the marching pulse, uilleann pipes and fiddle trade the
melody over a full-band build, and the whole thing resolves into a
massed, pub-session style crowd singalong — verses carried by a lead
voice, chorus roared back by thousands, exactly how the terraces already
sing.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Bodhrán alone, then uilleann pipes rise underneath)
Up the Boys in Green, up the Boys in Green...

[Verse 1]
From Dalymount to the Aviva, the same old song we sing,
Every county sends its heart to stand behind the green,
The fiddle and the bodhrán beat the way our fathers knew,
Sé mo laoch, mo ghile mear — this pride is nothing new.

[Pre-Chorus]
Tá muid linn go deo, through the wind and through the rain,
We follow every fixture, win or lose, again and again.

[Chorus] — sung terrace-style, call and response
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Up the Boys in Green, we'll sing it night and day!
Ireland! Ireland!
Tír na nÓg could not outshine the green we wear today!

[Verse 2]
No matter what the scoreboard says when the final whistle blows,
The Boys in Green walk off the pitch to a roar that always grows,
From Cork to Donegal, from Dublin to Mayo,
Is cuma cén stáisiún — one song, everywhere we go.

[Chorus]
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Up the Boys in Green, we'll sing it night and day!
Ireland! Ireland!
Tír na nÓg could not outshine the green we wear today!

[Bridge]
Give me a jersey and a session in full swing,
The pipes, the drum, the whole stand on its feet to sing,
Amhrán ár muintire, passed on down the line,
This is more than football — this is Ireland's time.

[Final Chorus]
Ólé, Ólé, Ólé, Ólé!
Up the Boys in Green — Ireland, hear us say!

[Outro]
Suas leis! Up the Boys in Green!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built around "The Boys in Green," the real, affectionate nickname for the
Republic of Ireland national football team, and the terrace chant "Ólé
Ólé Ólé," a genuine fixture of Irish away support since the Italia '90
era. The style draws on Irish trad session music (bodhrán, uilleann
pipes, fiddle) rather than generic rock, in the same communal
crowd-singing spirit as real Irish football and rugby anthems like
"Fields of Athenry." Irish-language phrases (tá muid linn go deo — "we
are with you forever," is cuma cén stáisiún — "no matter the ground,"
suas leis — "up with it/them") are woven through the mostly-English lyric
to reflect how bilingual pride actually surfaces in Irish sporting
culture.
