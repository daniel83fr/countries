JAMAICA
Official language: English (Jamaican Patois widely spoken)

TITLE: "One Yaad, One Fyah" (One Home, One Fire)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Reggae-driven stadium anthem. One-drop reggae riddim carries the whole
groove — the genre Jamaica gave the entire world — nyabinghi hand-drum
accents, opening into a full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals, sprint-
starting-blocks energy folded into the beat.

LYRICS (Jamaican Patois):

[Intro]
(A one-drop riddim locks in under a nyabinghi drum pattern)
Jamaica... one fyah...

[Verse 1]
Blue Mountain coffee grow slow but it grow the best,
Sprinter tek off like lightning, beat every single test,
Bob sing "one love" long before the whole worl' understand,
An' every match we play carry riddim from dis lan'.

[Pre-Chorus]
From di mountain top a mawnin' to di stadium packed tight,
One yaad, one people, movin' as one light.

[Chorus] — the hook: "One Yaad, One Fyah" means "one home, one fire"
One yaad, one fyah! One yaad, one fyah!
Reggae inna wi soul, we nah go tire,
One yaad, one fyah! One yaad, one fyah!
Jamaica, Jamaica, we di whole world admire.

[Verse 2]
Reggae Boyz run di field like sprinter run di line,
Every likkle island boy dream 'bout dis exact same time,
No matter weh di bigger nation dem seh 'bout we,
We small islan' produce di fastest man to ever be.

[Chorus]
One yaad, one fyah! One yaad, one fyah!
Reggae inna wi soul, we nah go tire,
One yaad, one fyah! One yaad, one fyah!
Jamaica, Jamaica, we di whole world admire.

[Bridge]
Mountain coffee an' a sprinter's blazing pace,
A likkle islan' wid a giant kinda grace —
One-drop riddim an' a nyabinghi drum,
Dis a di anthem weh di whole worl' come from.

[Final Chorus]
One yaad, one fyah! One yaad, one fyah!
Jamaica, Jamaica, we di whole world admire.

[Outro]
Jamaica! One love! One fyah!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on reggae music, Jamaica's global musical gift, and the country's
extraordinary sprinting legacy, which has produced the fastest human
beings in recorded history. "The Reggae Boyz" is the national football
team's real nickname. Written in Jamaican Patois, the language most
Jamaicans actually speak day to day. Have a native Patois speaker review
phrasing before recording.
