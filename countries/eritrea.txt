ERITREA
Working language: Tigrinya (also Arabic, English)

TITLE: "Eritrea, Kedem" (Eritrea, Forward)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Krar-driven Red Sea anthem. Krar (six-stringed lyre) carrying the melodic
hook over a kebero hand-drum rhythm built to mimic a cyclist's steady
cadence — a nod to Eritrea's real cycling culture — Art Deco Asmara street
ambience, opening into a full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Krar lyre figure over a steady, pedaling kebero drum pulse)
Eritrea... kedem...

[Verse 1]
Asmara's Art Deco cafés still hum with the same old song,
Bicycles climb the highland roads where riders have trained so long,
Red Sea coral waters keep a coastline no one can erase,
Every hill this nation's climbed has carved us a harder pace.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the highland cycling road to the coffee cup's warm sway,
One young nation, hard-earned, riding into the day.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Tigrinya + English)
Eritrea, kedem! Eritrea, kedem!
(Eritrea, forward! Eritrea, forward!)
Hade libbi — Eritrea, kedem!
(One heart — Eritrea, forward!)

[Verse 2]
The cyclist doesn't stop for hills, he learns to love the climb,
Every pedal stroke is proof of patience, proof of time,
Doesn't matter how the years of struggle carved this land,
We built endurance into something we could understand.

[Chorus]
Eritrea, kedem! Eritrea, kedem!
Hade libbi — Eritrea, kedem!

[Bridge]
Every hill a lesson, every ride a test,
Independence taught us how to give our best —
Krar string and pedal stroke, side by side,
This is the anthem of a highland pride.

[Final Chorus — full crowd]
Eritrea, kedem! Eritrea, kedem!
Hade libbi — Eritrea, kedem!
Kedem! Kedem!

[Outro — chant tag]
Eritrea! Kedem!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on Eritrea's genuine, world-renowned cycling culture (a legacy of
Italian colonial-era roads that turned into one of Africa's strongest
cycling traditions) and Asmara's UNESCO-listed Art Deco architecture. Have
a native Tigrinya speaker review phrasing before recording.
