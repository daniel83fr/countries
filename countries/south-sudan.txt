SOUTH SUDAN
Official language: English

TITLE: "South Sudan, Rise"

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Nilotic cattle-camp stadium anthem. Bul drum and cattle-bell percussion
patterns (echoing real cattle-camp rhythms) open the verses, building with
gourd rattles and hand-claps into a full-band chorus with massed crowd
vocals along the White Nile.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Bul drum pattern rises under cattle-bell percussion)
South Sudan... rise...

[Verse 1]
The White Nile carries stories older than the newest flag,
Cattle camps still glow at dusk with fires that never lag,
Youngest nation on the earth but oldest in the songs we sing,
And every match-day gathering carries everything.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the cattle camp at sunset to the stadium's roaring crowd,
One young nation, hard-won, standing proud.

[Chorus]
South Sudan, rise! South Sudan, rise!
Youngest flag but the oldest fire in our eyes,
South Sudan, rise! South Sudan, rise!
One people, many rivers, under the same skies.

[Verse 2]
Every cattle horn still curves the way our pride was always shown,
A generation raised on struggle built a nation of its own,
Doesn't matter what the years of hardship tried to weigh,
We are still the people the White Nile carries every day.

[Chorus]
South Sudan, rise! South Sudan, rise!
Youngest flag but the oldest fire in our eyes,
South Sudan, rise! South Sudan, rise!
One people, many rivers, under the same skies.

[Bridge]
Cattle camp fire glowing through the night,
A young nation's courage, patient, steady, bright —
Drumbeat and river current, side by side,
This is the anthem of a nation's pride.

[Final Chorus]
South Sudan, rise! South Sudan, rise!
One people, many rivers, under the same skies.

[Outro]
South Sudan! Rise! Rise!

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on cattle-camp culture, central to Dinka, Nuer, and other Nilotic
peoples' identity, and the White Nile, and South Sudan's real status as
the world's youngest country, having gained independence in 2011. English
is South Sudan's official language, so the full lyric is written directly
in it.
