BANGLADESH
Official language: Bengali (Bangla)

TITLE: "Bangladesh Jitbe" (Bangladesh Will Win)

STYLE / GENRE TAG:
Bengali folk-pop stadium anthem. Dhol and dhak drums driving the rhythm,
ektara drone and bansuri flute in the verses for a bhatiali river-song
feel, building into a full-band chorus with massed crowd vocals — the
sound of a Sher-e-Bangla National Stadium crowd turned into a record.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
(Dhak drum roll over river sounds and a flute line)
Bangladesh... aaj Bangladesh...

[Verse 1]
Green paddy fields bending low before the rain,
The Padma and the Meghna carry our joy and our pain,
A red sun rises on a flag we bled to keep,
Eleven men can make a hundred million weep.

[Pre-Chorus]
From the tea hills of Sylhet to the Bay's open door,
Every heartbeat in this country wants one thing more.

[Chorus] (viral hook, Bangla + English)
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!
(Bangladesh, Bangladesh, Bangladesh will win again!)
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!

[Verse 2]
The Tigers walk out and the whole ground turns to fire,
A rickshaw puller and a minister share the same desire,
Doesn't matter the scoreline written in the past,
We built a country out of harder odds than that.

[Chorus]
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!

[Bridge]
We are the delta, we are the river's will,
Bent by the storm but never standing still —
Green for the fields and red for the sun,
This is the story of a war we won.

[Final Chorus — full stadium]
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!
Bangladesh, Bangladesh, jitbe abar Bangladesh!

[Outro — chant tag]
Joy Bangladesh! Joy Bangladesh!
(Victory to Bangladesh!)

CULTURAL NOTE:
Built on real, specific national symbols — the red-on-green flag (sun of
independence on a field of green), the great rivers Padma and Meghna, the
tea gardens of Sylhet, and the near-religious following of the national
cricket team ("the Tigers"). "Joy Bangladesh" is a genuine, widely used
patriotic phrase. Have a native Bangla speaker/poet polish the verse
grammar and meter before recording — Bangla lyric-writing rewards internal
rhyme that a non-native draft can't fully capture.
